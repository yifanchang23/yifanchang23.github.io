Working Paper

Affect-Cued Selective Recall: Evidence from Employee Retrospective Wage Evaluation Under Inflation, with Xing Liu (opens in a new tab)

Draft available upon request

Read abstract

We study people's recall and retrospective evaluation of past experiences under affect-cued selective recall. Specifically, contexts with negative affective valence cue negative recollections, which then lead to negative retrospective evaluations. Using 1 million Glassdoor reviews from over 4,000 U.S. firms, we find that exposure to high inflation at the time of review, as the retrieval context carrying negative affective valence, negatively affects employees' retrospective wage evaluations. Meanwhile, inflation experienced prior to the review establishes an affective norm that mitigates the negative affective impact of high inflation exposure. For former employees, higher inflation experienced post employment lowers their retrospective wage evaluations, consistent with recent experiences interfering with accurate recall of earlier ones. Open-ended review texts support the mechanism of affect-cued selective recall. Importantly, this memory process extends to individual's forward-looking belief formation: high inflation exposure pessimizes employees' beliefs about their employers' near-term business outlook, partly through distorted retrospective evaluations. Finally, these crowdsourced employee evaluations enter market-wide information aggregation and influence firm-level outcomes from employee productivity to labor flows, with these effects varying across information recipients and labor market conditions.

Presented at: 6th Research in Behavioral Finance Conference, 8th Behavioral Finance Group Meeting, Tsinghua PBCSF.

Prior Work (before PhD)

Directional Trust and Venture Capital Investments: Evidence from China

Undergraduate Thesis

Read abstract

Bottazzi et al. (2016) show that generalized trust between nations facilitates cross-border venture capital investment but lowers investors' participation threshold, thereby inducing investment in marginal companies with lower success probabilities and impairing performance. However, the symmetric, nation-level trust measure obscures potentially asymmetric effects of directional trust between VC investors and investees. In this paper, we exploit the asymmetric structure of inter-provincial trust in China to examine how directional trust between VC investors and investees shapes VC deal structure and performance. Using a comprehensive sample of cross-province VC deals in China from 2000 to 2022 and a survey-based measure of inter-provincial trust from the Chinese Entrepreneurs Survey System (CESS), we find that higher investee-to-investor trust is associated with larger investment amounts, earlier investment stages, and a lower syndication likelihood, consistent with entrepreneurs being more willing to cede bargaining power and accept concentrated ownership when they trust their investors. Conversely, higher investor-to-investee trust predicts higher pre-money valuations and worse investment performance, consistent with investors' trust-induced generosity in deal pricing and laxity in ex-ante screening. Our findings are robust to instrumenting directional trust with genetic and surname distances across provinces.
